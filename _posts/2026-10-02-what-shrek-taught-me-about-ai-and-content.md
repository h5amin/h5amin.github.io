Every time I open the internet, I ask myself: what the actual *fuck* is going on?

Overnight, everyone became an AI expert.

And not in a "here's something actually useful" way. More in a ***I WILL SHOVE AI DOWN YOUR THROAT NO MATTER THE COST*** way.

Panic inducing.<br>
Slop producing.<br>
Creativity destroying.<br>
Soul crushing.

I've been on my own journey with AI, and I'm not saying that it has no place in content. It absolutely does. I use it every day.

**But the advice floating around out there is selling a fantasy:**

- *Scale quality content 4x*
- *Write high-converting emails 10x faster*
- *Increase AI visibility by 2000000001%*

## AI can't write Shrek. But it *can* help you produce Puss in Boots.

Here's the mental model I want you to take away: **your job is to create Shrek. AI's job is to repurpose your genius into a spin-off like Puss in Boots.**

Shrek (the original, irreplaceable, deeply weird ogre) is your core content. Your thought leadership. Your perspective pieces. Your brand voice. The stuff that only *you* can make because it comes from your lived experiences, your opinions, your hot takes, and your specific way of seeing the world.

**AI doesn't have any of that.** It can't replicate the feeling of being a solo marketer expected to 3x MRR while wearing seventeen different hats. It doesn't know what it felt like when your campaign flopped, or when one weird idea you threw at the wall became your highest-performing campaign ever.

What it *can* do is spin up a very charming, very useful sidekick. Once you've done the hard work of building your foundation (your Shrek), that's when you're allowed to lean on AI to help you move faster. Repurpose, rephrase, adapt, expand. Puss in Boots is great! But he's the sidekick. He's not what brings people back every time a new movie is created.

## Start with quality. Then scale quantity.

Shrek 1 made $484M. The success of this movie set the foundation for Shrek 2 to be the most successful movie of the bunch, hitting about $928 million. After Shrek 2, each sequential movie made a little less than the one before. **But here's the thing: they were all still wildly profitable.**

{% include shrek-chart.html %}

The franchise kept printing money because the foundation was so strong that even the spin-offs had gravity.

That's the move. You need quality *and* quantity. But the order matters.

Create something worth scaling first. Figure out what your Shrek is (what's your angle, your voice, your POV that no one else has?) and *then* let AI help you do more of it, faster. Repurpose it. Adapt it. Expand it into its own little universe.

Because a hundred mediocre pieces will never outperform ten great ones. But ten great ones? Those can fuel a whole franchise.

And a thousand AI-generated posts that sound like everyone else's AI-generated posts? That's not a content strategy. That's noise.

Your audience is smart. Give them something worth reading.

## So, what's your Shrek?

That's the question worth sitting with. Not "how do I use AI?" but "what do I have to say that's actually worth saying, and how do I protect that as I scale?"

Figure that out, and AI becomes a genuinely useful co-pilot. Skip it, and you're just another Far Far Away fairy tale blending into the background.
